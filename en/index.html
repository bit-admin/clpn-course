Course Overview

Course objectives. When you finish this course, you will be able to:

  1. Explain in plain language what a cross-lagged panel network (CLPN) is, which questions it can answer and which it cannot;
  2. Carry out a complete CLPN analysis in R on your own: data cleaning, regularized estimation, visualization, centrality analysis and stability checks;
  3. Read published CLPN papers critically and spot common over-interpretations;
  4. Write Methods and Results sections to academic standards.

Why does social work need it? Social work and psychology research often describes a person with a scale's "total score" (a total depression score, a total loneliness score). But in practice we deal with specific problems: not sleeping well, having no energy, feeling there is no one to talk to... A CLPN lets us look at the item/symptom level and across time: which early problem best predicts later change in the other problems? This gives clues for designing more targeted interventions, but what it gives is predictive relationships; testing causation still takes intervention or experimental studies.

Teaching Approach

Every lesson is an interactive HTML page (in the lessons/ folder; click a lesson's "Open" button below to go in). All 47 lessons share the same layout and navigation and the same five-part structure:

① Intuition

First, social work scenarios, diagrams and small simulations you can drag give you a feel for "what this is doing"; no formulas yet.

② Essential Math

Only the 1–2 formulas you need to understand the idea, each symbol explained; any use of calculus is pointed out.

③ R Code

Copyable code with a comment on every line: run it on small data first, then on the course data; common errors are explained.

④ Concept Check

Multiple-choice, true/false and "predict the output" questions throughout the lesson, with instant feedback; a wrong answer gets a hint first rather than the answer.

⑤ Exercises & Self-Assessment

Do each lesson's exercises as an R script, a Quarto report or a short piece of writing, then check yourself against the reference answers (code solutions in R/solutions/) and the scoring points; you can revise and redo them. Before starting the next lesson, answer the previous lesson's self-test questions without looking at the answers.

Principles

  • Spiral: the same idea (such as "controlling for other variables") comes back in multiple regression, partial correlation and the CLPN, one level deeper each time.
  • One running case: the whole course uses simulated "loneliness ↔ depression" data whose true structure is known, and ends with your own analysis in the capstone project.
  • Say it before you compute it: explain every result in one sentence you could say to a social work colleague, and avoid over-causal wording.
  • Getting stuck is allowed: when you ask for help, include your code, the error message and your own guess (a minimal reproducible example); doing this is itself a skill to practice.
