M0 module quiz

Take this after Lessons 0.1 and 0.2. 6 questions, about 10 minutes. It checks whether you can see the route through the whole course and the difference between the network view and the "total-score view."

Auto-graded questions

The course teaches statistical foundations (M1) and regression and LASSO (M3) before CLPN (M7). What is the main reason?

  • It is not because it is easier, but because you will need it later.
  • Correct. M1 statistics and M3 regression are the "engine parts" of CLPN.
  • This has nothing to do with the order of the course.
  • R is learned alongside statistics from Week 2.
Each module supplies one skill the final CLPN analysis needs; see the course map in 0.1.

Every lesson is laid out in the same order. Which order is correct?

  • Wrong order.
  • Correct. First build a picture, then write it as formulas, then work hands-on.
  • Wrong order.
  • All lessons share the same structure.
A fixed structure tells you how to use each part; see 0.1.

Xiao Li has lately had insomnia, fatigue and poor concentration. How would the "common cause" view and the "network" view each explain this?

  • They start from different assumptions.
  • Correct.
  • The network view is precisely about the relationships among symptoms.
  • In the common cause view, the latent "depression" causes each symptom.
For the contrast between the latent variable (common cause) view and the network view, see 0.2.

Two clients both score 12 on a depression scale. Why shouldn't a social worker look only at the total score?

  • It is not a calculation problem.
  • Correct.
  • There is no such rule.
  • Total scores are also common in practice; they just lose information.
Looking only at the total score loses the information about which symptoms are present and how they combine; see 0.2.

A study finds that "loneliness at the first survey predicts depression at the second survey six months later (even after controlling for depression at the first survey)." What is this kind of relationship called?

  • A contemporaneous correlation is the relationship between two variables at the same time point.
  • Correct.
  • Autoregression is earlier A predicting later A itself.
  • A predictive relationship is not proven causation.
"Cross" means across variables and "lagged" means across time; see 0.2.

A CLPN study reports a strong edge "loneliness item A → depression item B." Which conclusion can a CLPN support?

  • This is a causal claim, and it is also applied to an individual.
  • A CLPN cannot establish causes of a disorder.
  • Correct. This is a group-level predictive relationship.
  • A strong edge in one direction does not mean the reverse direction is absent.
A CLPN answers "who predicts whom," not "who causes whom"; see 0.2.

When you finish

If you got at least 70% right on the first try, move on to the next module; if not, follow the links in the explanations of the questions you missed, review those lessons, and take the quiz again in a few days. Write your first-try score in your study log. For how scoring works and the rubrics, see the assessments page.
