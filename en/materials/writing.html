Writing Workshop: Methods, Results and Limitations

Four "before → after" examples; click a sentence to see why it is written that way. Each example ends with a short question and a rewriting task. Links Lesson 9.3 (reporting standards) and Lesson 10.3 (writing the results).

How to use this page

This page links 9.3 Reporting standards and 10.3 Writing the results. The four parts of a paper that most often go wrong are data and measures, statistical analysis, results, and limitations. For each one you get a "before" and an "after" example:

  1. Read the "before" version first and find the problems yourself.
  2. Then read the "after" version and click the sentences with a dashed underline to see why they are written that way.
  3. Answer a short question, then do the rewriting task (the model answer and a 0–3 rubric are folded away below it).

All examples use the course's simulated data. The numbers in the results example are read automatically from R/output/, so they match what you get when you run the course scripts. When you have finished writing, go through Cheat Sheet 4 · Reporting checklist item by item.

Example 1: Data and measures

Before

We collected data from 800 people and measured loneliness and depression. The data quality was very good, with no problems.

After

This study used the simulated panel data provided with the course (N = 800 community adults aged 18–85), with 3 waves 6 months apart.Loneliness was measured with 4 items (modeled on the UCLA Loneliness Scale, scored 1–4) and depression with 5 items (modeled on the PHQ-9, scored 0–3); each item is one node in the network.There were no missing data; a real study should report the number of people who completed each wave, the attrition rate, and how those who stayed and those who dropped out compare on the main wave-1 variables.

The "before" paragraph leaves out one design detail that a CLPN must report: how much time passed between two measurements. What does the course call this? (1–2 words)

In English it is called the lag or time interval.
The time interval (lag) affects which effects you can see; see 4.1 and 5.4.

Rewriting task 1

Rewrite this sentence so that others can check it (2–3 sentences): "We ran several surveys in a community using a common depression scale."

Model answer and rubric

Example: "We recruited residents aged 60 and over from 3 communities in one city (N = 412) and surveyed them in 3 waves, in March 2025, September 2025 and March 2026 (6 months apart). Depressive symptoms were measured with the first 8 items of the PHQ-9 (scored 0–3) … 78% completed wave 3; those who dropped out had slightly higher wave-1 loneliness scores (see Table S1)." (Fill in the numbers from your own study.)

LevelCriteria
3States the sample, recruitment, number of waves and interval, scale name and scoring, and attrition
2One or two of these are missing
1Still vague; only one item added
0No answer, or the rewrite still has the main problems of the original

Example 2: Statistical analysis

Before

We used network analysis to study the relationship between loneliness and depression, estimated the network with LASSO, and tested its stability.

After

We estimated a cross-lagged panel network (CLPN): for each T2 item we ran a separate LASSO regression with all 9 T1 items as predictors (Wysocki et al., 2025).All items were z-standardized before the analysis; λ was chosen by 10-fold cross-validation (lambda.min), with random seed 2026; a sensitivity analysis used lambda.1se instead and added gender and age (unpenalized) as covariates.Edge accuracy was assessed with 1000 nonparametric bootstrap resamples, and the stability of out- and in-expected influence with the case-dropping bootstrap and the CS-coefficient (Epskamp et al., 2018).The analysis was run in R (versions in the supplementary material) with glmnet and bootnet; the code is on OSF.

The "before" version only says "estimated the network with LASSO" and does not say how λ was chosen. What is the rule the course's main analysis uses when choosing λ by cross-validation? (Give its name in R.)

The other, more conservative rule is called lambda.1se.
The difference between the two rules is explained in 7.2. The methods section must say which one you used, and what the sensitivity analysis used.

Rewriting task 2

Write a statistical analysis paragraph for your capstone project (4–6 sentences) that covers at least: the model, the λ rule and number of folds, the random seed, covariates, accuracy and stability checks, and software.

Model answer and rubric

Use the "after" version above as a model, then check each point against the reporting checklist.

Example 3: Results

Before

The results show that loneliness caused depression. "No one understands me" is the core symptom, because its centrality is the highest. All edges were very stable.

After

In the loneliness → depression direction, the strongest cross-lagged edge was T1 "No one understands me" → T2 "Fatigue" (B = 0.108, bootstrap interval [0.011, 0.180]).That is, controlling for all T1 items, participants who more often felt "No one understands me" at wave 1 tended to report more "Fatigue" six months later."No one understands me" had the highest out-expected influence (0.61), but out-EI was not stable enough (CS = 0.20, unstable, < 0.25), so its ranking is not interpreted; for in-EI, CS = 0.52 (just at 0.5, marginally stable).Pairwise difference tests among the 10 strongest cross-lagged edges: all non-significant (every interval includes 0); so the ranking of the strong edges should also be read with caution.

Which word in the first sentence of the "before" version is causal language? (Give the word as it appears in the text.)

A CLPN can only say "predict".
"Cause", "lead to", "drive" and "make" are all causal language; see 5.4 and 7.3.

Rewriting task 3

Rewrite this sentence as a careful statement of results (1–2 sentences): "Fatigue has the highest in-expected influence, which shows that fatigue is the end result caused by loneliness."

Model answer and rubric

Example: "'Fatigue' had the highest in-expected influence (in-EI = 0.40, CS = 0.52), meaning it was the item best predicted by the other T1 items; this is a predictive relationship over time and does not show that fatigue is 'caused' by loneliness."

LevelCriteria
3Gives the number and its stability, says "predicted" rather than "caused", and makes no causal or individual-level claim
2Causal language removed, but the number or the stability is missing
1Still makes a causal claim
0No answer, or the rewrite still has the main problems of the original

Example 4: Limitations

Before

This study proved a causal relationship between loneliness and depression. Its only limitation is that the sample was not large enough.

After

First, a standard CLPN does not separate between-person and within-person effects; the cross-lagged edges may partly reflect stable differences between people rather than processes of change within a person (Hamaker et al., 2015).Second, the results show only predictive relationships over time; unmeasured confounders (such as financial strain in the household) may affect both loneliness and depression.Third, an interval of 6 months may miss faster processes of change; we did not test measurement invariance, so whether the items meant the same thing at every wave remains to be confirmed.Fourth, out-EI was not stable enough, and conclusions based on it need to be replicated in a larger sample.

Which word in the first sentence of the "before" version states the conclusion too strongly? (Give the word as it appears in the text.)

An observational study can rarely "____" anything.
Use "suggests" or "is consistent with" instead of "proved"; see 10.3.

Rewriting task 4

Write 3 limitations for your capstone project (1–2 sentences each), covering at least two of the three types (within- vs. between-person, causality, measurement), each tied to your actual results.

Model answer and rubric

Use the "after" version above as a model. For each limitation, say what the problem is → how it might affect your conclusions → how future work could do better.

LevelCriteria
3All 3 are specific, tied to the results, and cover at least two types
2Three limitations, but one is generic
1Only one or two, and very general
0No answer, or the rewrite still has the main problems of the original

After writing

  • Go through Cheat Sheet 4 · Reporting checklist item by item.
  • Search the whole text for "cause", "lead to", "prove", "core symptom" and "drive", and fix each one.
  • Every number should trace back to a file in R/output/ (this is how the results draft in Lesson 10.3 is generated automatically).
  • Reporting standards for network analysis: Burger et al. (2023), doi.org/10.1037/met0000471. More literature in the reading pack.
