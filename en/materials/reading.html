Paper Reading Pack

12 selected papers grouped by topic, each with a reading question and companion lessons (DOI links; papers marked “open access” can be downloaded directly).

How to read

  • This page gives links only (DOIs), no PDFs. Papers marked "open access" can be downloaded directly; for subscription journals, go through your university library, or look for the authors' own copy on their homepage or a preprint server.
  • Each paper comes with a reading question and companion lessons. Finish the companion lessons first, then read with the question in mind. You don't need to follow every formula; it is enough if you can answer the question in your own words.
  • When you finish, write three sentences in your study log: what question the paper answers, what method it uses, and what it suggests for your capstone project.

Getting started: what is a CLPN?

  1. Wysocki, A., McCarthy, I., van Bork, R., Cramer, A. O. J., & Rhemtulla, M. (2025). Cross-lagged panel networks. advances.in/psychology, 2, e739621. https://doi.org/10.56296/aip00037 (CC BY 4.0, open access)

    Reading question: Why do the authors say that CLPN edges are "regression coefficients" rather than "correlations"? What question does an autoregressive edge answer, and what question does a cross-lagged edge answer?

    Companion lessons: 7.1 Model Logic of CLPN: Unpacking CLPM to the Item Level, 7.2 Nodewise CLPN Estimation with glmnet

  2. Epskamp, S., Borsboom, D., & Fried, E. I. (2018). Estimating psychological networks and their accuracy: A tutorial paper. Behavior Research Methods, 50(1), 195–212. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-017-0862-1 (CC BY 4.0, open access)

    Reading question: What question does the edge-weight bootstrap answer, and what question does the case-dropping bootstrap answer? Where do the CS-coefficient lines of 0.25 and 0.5 come from?

    Companion lessons: 8.2 Edge-Weight Accuracy: bootnet and a Custom CLPN Estimation Function, 8.3 Case-Dropping Bootstrap and the CS-Coefficient

Empirical examples: how to write up a CLPN

  1. Funkhouser, C. J., Chacko, A. A., Correa, K. A., Kaiser, A. J. E., & Shankman, S. A. (2021). Unique longitudinal relationships between symptoms of psychopathology in youth: A cross-lagged panel network analysis in the ABCD study. Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry, 62(2), 184–194. https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.13256 (subscription journal (available through a university library))

    Reading question: How do the authors handle replication across the two intervals T1→T2 and T2→T3? Which stability indices do they use? Does the conclusion avoid causal language?

    Companion lessons: 9.1 How to Read an Empirical CLPN Paper

  2. Grygiel, P., Opozda-Suder, S., & Dolata, R. (2026). The longitudinal interplay between loneliness and depressive symptoms during late childhood: Cross-lagged panel network analyses. European Journal of Investigation in Health, Psychology and Education, 16(6), 78. https://doi.org/10.3390/ejihpe16060078 (CC BY 4.0, open access)

    Reading question: The sample comes from the NLSY79 Children and Young Adults survey (NLSY79-CY): N = 4,333 children aged 10–12, with only two time points. How are the item-level relationships between loneliness and depression similar to, and different from, the structure of the course's simulated data? With only two waves, what can the authors test and what can they not (for example, can they check replication in T2→T3)? How does the age of the sample affect how far the conclusions generalize?

    Companion lessons: 9.1 How to Read an Empirical CLPN Paper, 10.3 Writing the Results and Presenting Them

  3. Fried, E. I., Papanikolaou, F., & Epskamp, S. (2022). Mental health and social contact during the COVID-19 pandemic: An ecological momentary assessment study. Clinical Psychological Science, 10(2), 340–354. https://doi.org/10.1177/21677026211017839 (CC BY 4.0, open access)

    Reading question: How do the questions that intensive longitudinal (EMA) data answer differ from those of three-wave panel data? What does "within-person" mean here?

    Companion lessons: 5.3 RI-CLPM: Separating Stable Trait Differences from Within-Person Fluctuations, 7.5 Design Choices and Extensions: Multi-Wave Data, Covariates, and Within-Person Disentanglement

Model critique: within-person vs. between-person

  1. Hamaker, E. L., Kuiper, R. M., & Grasman, R. P. P. P. (2015). A critique of the cross-lagged panel model. Psychological Methods, 20(1), 102–116. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0038889 (subscription journal)

    Reading question: Why do stable individual differences make cross-lagged coefficients "look like" effects? What does the RI-CLPM add? What does this mean for how you interpret a CLPN?

    Companion lessons: 5.3 RI-CLPM: Separating Stable Trait Differences from Within-Person Fluctuations, 7.5 Design Choices and Extensions: Multi-Wave Data, Covariates, and Within-Person Disentanglement

Measurement invariance (optional Lesson 7.6)

  1. Putnick, D. L., & Bornstein, M. H. (2016). Measurement invariance conventions and reporting: The state of the art and future directions for psychological research. Developmental Review, 41, 71–90. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dr.2016.06.004 (subscription journal)

    Reading question: What does each of configural, metric (weak) and scalar (strong) invariance constrain? How are the model comparisons usually reported in papers?

    Companion lessons: 7.6 Measurement Invariance and Sample Size Planning

  2. Cheung, G. W., & Rensvold, R. B. (2002). Evaluating goodness-of-fit indexes for testing measurement invariance. Structural Equation Modeling, 9(2), 233–255. https://doi.org/10.1207/S15328007SEM0902_5 (subscription journal)

    Reading question: Why look at ΔCFI instead of the chi-square difference test? What is the basis for the ΔCFI ≤ 0.01 line?

    Companion lessons: 7.6 Measurement Invariance and Sample Size Planning

  3. Chen, F. F. (2007). Sensitivity of goodness of fit indexes to lack of measurement invariance. Structural Equation Modeling, 14(3), 464–504. https://doi.org/10.1080/10705510701301834 (subscription journal)

    Reading question: What cut-offs are recommended for ΔRMSEA and ΔSRMR? How does sample size affect them?

    Companion lessons: 7.6 Measurement Invariance and Sample Size Planning

Group comparison and sample size (optional Lesson 7.7, advanced)

  1. van Borkulo, C. D., van Bork, R., Boschloo, L., Kossakowski, J. J., Tio, P., Schoevers, R. A., Borsboom, D., & Waldorp, L. J. (2023). Comparing network structures on three aspects: A permutation test. Psychological Methods, 28(6), 1273–1285. https://doi.org/10.1037/met0000476 (subscription journal)

    Reading question: Which three aspects does the Network Comparison Test (NCT) test? How should you set the number of permutations and the correction for multiple comparisons?

    Companion lessons: 7.7 Between-Group Comparisons: Are Two Group CLPNs Identical?

  2. Constantin, M. A., Schuurman, N. K., & Vermunt, J. K. (2026). A general Monte Carlo method for sample size analysis in the context of network models. Psychological Methods, 31(3), 385–405. https://doi.org/10.1037/met0000555 (APA open access)

    Reading question: Why does the sample size for a network model have to be estimated by repeated simulation? How is "power" defined here?

    Companion lessons: 7.6 Measurement Invariance and Sample Size Planning, 7.7 Between-Group Comparisons: Are Two Group CLPNs Identical?

Reporting standards

Reporting standards for psychological network analyses in cross-sectional data. Psychological Methods, 28(4), 806–824. https://doi.org/10.1037/met0000471 (subscription journal)
