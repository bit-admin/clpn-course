Pre-test: check your starting point

Spend about 20 minutes on this before you start. The pre-test is ungraded: recommended, but optional. It only shows your starting point: arithmetic and proportions, reading tables, statistical intuition, terminology, and basic computer skills. When you finish, use the table at the bottom of the page to choose your study pace.

How to take it

  • Answer from what you know now: no looking things up, and no tools other than a calculator. If you don't know, guess. A wrong guess is fine; the point of the pre-test is to find what you don't know yet.
  • Only your first answer to each question counts. The top of the page shows "(y correct on first try)"; write down y.

First, the 6 self-assessment items (ungraded)

On paper or in your study log, mark each statement "Yes" or "No":

  1. I have used Excel or similar software to calculate an average.
  2. I have heard of terms such as p-value and correlation coefficient.
  3. I can read a short English-language research abstract (with a dictionary if needed).
  4. I have written computer code of any kind (even if I only copied, pasted and ran it).
  5. I can find a downloaded file on my computer and unzip an archive.
  6. I can reliably set aside at least 6 hours a week for this course.

Auto-graded questions

A community center served 30 clients this month; 12 of them reported "often feeling lonely." What percentage of clients reported feeling lonely? (Enter a number only, e.g. 25)

Proportion = part ÷ whole; then multiply by 100.
12 ÷ 30 = 0.4, i.e. 40%. Proportions and percentages are the first step in descriptive statistics; see 1.2.

Five adolescents do 2, 4, 4, 6 and 9 hours of volunteer work per week. What is the average number of hours per person?

Mean = sum of all values ÷ number of people.
(2 + 4 + 4 + 6 + 9) ÷ 5 = 25 ÷ 5 = 5. For how to calculate a mean, see 1.2.

A depression scale has 9 items, each scored 0–3, and the total score is the sum of the 9 items. What is the highest possible total score?

Each item scores at most 3. How many items are there?
9 × 3 = 27. You need to know a scale's possible range to judge whether a score is high or low; for the difference between items and total scores, see 1.1.

The table shows how often 100 students at a school met friends last week: 0 times, 40 students; 1–2 times, 35; 3–5 times, 20; 6 or more times, 5. Which statement is most accurate?

  • Only 20 + 5 = 25 students met friends 3 or more times.
  • Correct. A shape that piles up on the left with a tail stretching to the right is called right-skewed.
  • 40 and 5 are very different.
  • You can see that the number of students falls as the number of meetings rises.
Reading a frequency table means looking at how many people fall into each category. This shape, many on the left and few on the right, is called right-skewed (positively skewed); see 1.2.

A questionnaire codes "Marital status: 1 = never married, 2 = married, 3 = divorced, 4 = widowed." Which of the following is the WRONG thing to do?

  • This is reasonable.
  • This is reasonable.
  • Correct, this is the wrong thing to do. The numbers are only labels for categories, not amounts, so their average means nothing.
  • This is reasonable.
A variable whose values only name categories is a nominal variable. The level of measurement decides which calculations make sense; see 1.1.

A line chart shows the yearly number of calls to a hotline from 2020 to 2023: 800, 1,200, 1,500 and 1,450. Which description is most accurate?

  • Calls rose over the first three years.
  • Correct.
  • The changes are clearly visible.
  • The peak was 1,500 in 2022.
When reading a chart, first check what the vertical axis shows, then look for the trend and turning points. For the basics of describing data, see 1.2.

Among the monthly salaries of 6 employees at an agency, one director earns far more than everyone else. Which number better describes "the typical employee's monthly salary"?

  • The mean is pulled up by that one very high value.
  • Correct.
  • The maximum only describes the director.
  • With an extreme value, the difference can be large.
When there are extreme values or the distribution is skewed, the median represents "the person in the middle" better than the mean; see 1.2.

A study finds that older adults who take part in more community activities have lower depression scores. Which statement is the most cautious?

  • "Reduces" is a causal claim, and this association alone cannot support it.
  • This is also a causal guess, and it has not been shown either.
  • Correct.
  • The study did find an association.
Correlation is not causation. A third variable that affects both is called a confounder; see 1.4.

A researcher randomly samples 500 of a city's 20,000 college students and computes their mean loneliness score. What is this mean of the 500 students called in statistics?

  • A parameter is the (usually unknown) value that describes all 20,000 students.
  • Correct. We use sample statistics to estimate population parameters.
  • The population is the 20,000 college students themselves.
  • No.
For population vs sample and parameter vs statistic, see 1.5.

"In months when ice cream sales are high, there are also more drownings," yet ice cream does not make people drown. What do statisticians call the "third variable" that drives both?

In hot weather, more people eat ice cream and more people go swimming. What is "the weather" called in statistics?
Weather (temperature) affects both, and a variable like that is called a confounder (confounding variable); see 1.4.

What is a single question on a questionnaire scale called in the research literature? (One word)

It starts with "i" and is contrasted with the scale score.
A single question is an item, the summed score is the scale score, and the psychological trait they aim to measure is a construct; see 1.1.

What is the statistical term for the degree to which two variables vary together?

A statistic that describes whether two variables change together.
Correlation describes how two variables vary together; it is usually expressed as Pearson's correlation coefficient r. See 1.4.

You downloaded the course archive clpn-course-en.zip. What should you do first, before using any of the files in it?

  • Changes made to files inside an archive are often not saved.
  • Correct. Open the files only after extracting them.
  • Never do this.
  • There is no need to.
Extract all course files before using them; see the learning guide and 2.4.

You created a folder called "course" inside your computer's "Documents" folder and put data.csv in it. Which of these is most likely the file's full path on Windows?

  • The order is reversed: a path goes from the outer folder to the inner one and ends with the file name.
  • Correct.
  • This is just a file name; it does not say which folder the file is in.
  • This is how a web address is written, not a path on your computer.
A path goes from the drive, folder by folder, down to the file. When R reads data, you have to tell it where the file is; see 2.4.

When you finish: choose your pace

Correct on first try (out of 14)Recommendation
6 or fewer, or "No" on self-assessment item 6Take the relaxed pace of about 19 weeks (add a buffer week after weeks 5, 8 and 13; see Recommended Pacing in the syllabus). Move on past M1 and M2 only after scoring at least 70% on their module quizzes.
7–11Follow the standard 16 weeks. Go more slowly through the lessons linked from the questions you missed, and work the examples once more.
12 or moreYou can consider the compressed pace of about 12 weeks, but do not compress R (M2) or CLPN (M7).

Write your first-try score and your 6 self-assessment answers in your study log. After Lesson 10.3, take the final exam; some of its questions test the same points as the pre-test, so you can compare and see your progress.
