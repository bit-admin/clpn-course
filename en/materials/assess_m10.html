M10 module quiz

Take this before you finalize your capstone project (Week 16 of the standard pace). 4 auto-graded questions, about 10 minutes. The capstone project itself is scored on the dimensions in the syllabus.

Auto-graded questions

Which of these is a suitable research question for a CLPN capstone project?

  • "Cause" is a causal statement; a CLPN cannot answer it.
  • Correct. Clear, not causal, and matched to what a CLPN can answer.
  • This is a cross-sectional question that mixes up the group and individual levels.
  • This is an intervention question, not a CLPN research question.
For how to write a research question, see 10.1.

A loneliness item is answered on a 1–4 scale (1 = never, 4 = often). Its wording is positive, "I have many people I can rely on," so it needs reverse scoring. A person answered "2." What is their score after reverse scoring?

Reverse scoring on a 1–4 scale: new score = (minimum + maximum) − original score.
(1 + 4) − 2 = 3. For reverse scoring, see 10.1.

When should the analysis plan (a simple preregistration) be written?

  • Then it no longer does what a preregistration is for.
  • Correct. Any later departure from the plan must be stated in the report.
  • No.
  • The timing is exactly what matters.
For what goes into an analysis plan (nodes, λ rule, seed, stability checks, sensitivity analyses) and when to write it, see 10.1.

Which sentence best belongs in the Limitations section of a capstone project?

  • This is a causal conclusion, and it is not a limitation.
  • Correct.
  • Every study has limitations.
  • A nonsignificant result is not in itself a problem with the method.
For writing the Limitations section, see 10.3 (and 7.5, 9.2).

When you finish

If you got at least 70% right on the first try, move on to the next module; if not, follow the links in the explanations of the questions you missed, review those lessons, and take the quiz again in a few days. Write your first-try score in your study log. For how scoring works and the rubrics, see the assessments page.
