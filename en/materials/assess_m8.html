M8 module quiz

Take this after Lessons 8.1–8.3 (Week 14 of the standard pace). 8 auto-graded questions + 1 short-answer question, about 20 minutes. You don't need to open R.

Auto-graded questions

You run a nonparametric bootstrap on a sample of N = 200. How is each bootstrap sample drawn?

  • The sample must be the same size as the original, and drawn with replacement.
  • Correct.
  • A bootstrap collects no new data.
  • That is closer to a permutation (7.7) than to a bootstrap.
For how the nonparametric bootstrap works, see 8.1.

You ran 1000 bootstrap samples and sorted the 1000 correlation coefficients from smallest to largest. Which value in the sorted list is the lower limit of the 95% percentile interval?

2.5% is cut off at each end. What is 2.5% of 1000?
1000 × 2.5% = 25, so the lower limit is the 25th value and the upper limit the 975th. See 8.1.

Why does network analysis particularly need accuracy (stability) checks?

  • That is not the main reason.
  • Correct.
  • Looks have nothing to do with it.
  • Quite the opposite: it is most needed when the sample is limited.
For why network analysis needs accuracy checks, see 8.1 (Epskamp et al., 2018).

A cross-lagged edge has an estimate of 0.08 and a bootstrap interval of [0.00, 0.17]. Which interpretation is most appropriate?

  • The lower limit is 0, so at least 2.5% of the bootstrap samples set it to 0; it is hardly "very stable."
  • Correct. A lower limit of 0 only tells you that at least 2.5% of the samples were 0; for the actual proportion, look at the proportion of zeros (prop0).
  • "Unstable" does not mean "certainly absent."
  • A wider interval means a less precise estimate.
For how to read edge-weight bootstrap intervals, see 8.2.

Why does the course say the bootstrap intervals of LASSO-estimated CLPN edges "cannot be treated as strict confidence intervals"?

  • Correct.
  • No number of samples fixes the bias that shrinkage causes.
  • Confidence intervals can be used for many statistics.
  • CLPN edges do have a direction, and that has nothing to do with the question.
For what to keep in mind about bootstrap intervals under regularized estimation, see 8.2.

The bootstrap intervals of two edges partly overlap. You want to know whether "edge A is significantly stronger than edge B." What should you do?

  • Overlapping intervals do not by themselves show that the difference is not significant.
  • Correct.
  • Separate p-values cannot compare two edges.
  • Irrelevant.
For the edge-weight difference test, see 8.2.

By the rule of thumb of Epskamp et al. (2018), the CS-coefficient of a centrality index should not be below what value?

There is also a "preferably above" value of 0.5.
CS should not be below 0.25 and preferably above 0.5. This is a rule of thumb based on simulation studies, not a mathematical theorem. See 8.3.

What does the case-dropping bootstrap do?

  • The dropping is random, not chosen by missingness.
  • Correct.
  • It drops people, not edges.
  • No.
For the case-dropping bootstrap and the CS-coefficient, see 8.3.

Short-answer questions

Short answer 1

A CLPN study reports: CS-coefficient of in-EI = 0.44, CS-coefficient of out-EI = 0.13. In the Discussion the authors write: "'No one understands me,' which has the highest out-EI, is the most important intervention target." In 3–4 sentences, evaluate this sentence and give the wording you would recommend.

Reference answer & rubric

The CS of out-EI is 0.13, below the minimum of 0.25, so the out-EI ranking is very unstable: drop a small random part of the sample and the top-ranked node may change. You therefore cannot pick out the "most important" node from the out-EI ranking. Even if it were stable, a high out-EI only means the node predicts many other nodes over time; it does not mean that intervening on it would improve the other symptoms (causal and intervention claims need experiments or intervention studies). The CS of in-EI is 0.44, so its ranking can be interpreted with caution (it lies between 0.25 and 0.5). Recommended wording: "The stability of out-EI was insufficient (CS = 0.13), and we do not interpret its ranking; the stability of in-EI was acceptable (CS = 0.44), ... and may serve as a reference for future research and follow-up monitoring."

LevelCriteria
3Points out that the out-EI CS is below 0.25 so the ranking cannot be interpreted, points out that "intervention target" over-reads the result in causal / intervention terms, and gives a reasonable rewrite
2Points out the low CS but not the problem with the intervention language (or vice versa); the rewrite is mostly reasonable
1Only says "the CS is a bit low," without explaining what follows or giving a rewrite
0No answer, or says the original has no problem
