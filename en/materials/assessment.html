Assessments: Pre-Test, Module Quizzes & Final Exam

The pre-test shows where you start, each module quiz checks one module, and the final exam covers the whole course. This page explains how to use them, how they are scored, and the scoring rubrics.

1. How to use the assessments

The course has three kinds of assessment. All of them are there to help you see clearly what you already know and what you don't yet:

  • Pre-test: take it once before you start, to see your starting point and choose between the 16-week pace and the more relaxed 19-week pace. The pre-test is ungraded: recommended, but optional.
  • Module quizzes: take one after you finish each module. The questions are new, not repeats of the Concept Check questions in the lessons.
  • Final exam: take it after Lesson 10.3. It covers the whole course, and some of its questions test the same points as the pre-test, so you can see your progress.

You answer the questions the same way as the quizzes in the lessons: a wrong answer first gets a hint, and after two wrong answers you can click "Show Answer & Explanation." The explanation links back to the lesson that teaches the point.

About the answers

The course is a set of offline web pages, and the quizzes are there to help you find your weak spots. Answer on your own first, then open the answers and explanations to check.

3. How scoring works

The top of each assessment page shows "Quiz score on this page: x / n correct (y correct on first try)." Your score is the number correct on the first try: if you needed a second try, the point is not yet solid.

  • Write each first-try score and the time you took in the weekly summary of your study log. Scores are not saved automatically and reset when you reload the page.
  • Once you get at least 70% of a module quiz right on the first try, you can move on to the next module; if not, follow the links in the explanations of the questions you missed, review, and take the quiz again a few days later.
  • Short-answer questions are not scored automatically: write your answer first, then open "Reference answer & rubric" and score yourself on the 0–3 scale. If you are not happy with it, rewrite it a few days later and score it again.
  • For how each part counts toward the overall grade, see Assessment and Feedback in the syllabus.
