Short-answer questions

Short answer 1

Read this made-up abstract and point out at least three things to question, explaining each in one sentence:

"We surveyed 120 college students in March 2023 and April 2023 (1-month interval) and estimated a CLPN with 20 nodes using lambda.min. 'Helplessness' had the highest out-EI and is the root cause of depression. All cases with missing data were deleted."
Reference answer & rubric

Things to question (any three are enough): (1) Sample size: 20 nodes give 380 cross-lagged edges, and 120 people are far too few; the estimates will be very unstable. (2) No stability checks: the out-EI ranking is interpreted without reporting bootstrap intervals or CS-coefficients. (3) Causal language: "root cause" is a causal conclusion, while a CLPN only gives prediction over time. (4) Missing data: cases with missing data were simply deleted, without reporting the missing proportion or an attrition analysis, which may cause bias. (5) Interval: whether a 1-month interval fits the process being studied needs a justification. (6) λ rule: lambda.min is lenient and gives more false positives, so a sensitivity analysis should be reported. (7) There is no mention that between-person and within-person effects are mixed together.

LevelCriteria
3Points out at least three problems, each with a correct reason (sample size / stability / causal language / missing data / interval / λ rule / within- vs between-person)
2Points out three problems, but one reason is inaccurate; or only two, with sound reasons
1Points out only one problem
0No answer, or none of the reasons hold
